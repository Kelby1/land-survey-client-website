03 / THE FIELDBOOK

An accurate service story, without the usual card grid

Survey work moves from records, to the field, to computation, to a usable plan.

ENTRY 01 Records first

Understand the property before putting instruments on the ground.

Typical intake may include a title or technical description, previous survey plans, tax or property records, location references, and authority documents when a representative is acting for the owner.

  • Title / technical description, when available
  • Previous approved or reference survey plans
  • Property location and access information
  • Owner / authorized representative contact details
  • Project purpose and required deliverables

Exact requirements vary by survey type, property status, and approving agency.

ENTRY 02 Measure the site

Observe boundaries, features, elevations, and control points in context.

Depending on scope, fieldwork can involve GNSS/GPS control, total station observations, leveling, conventional measurements, imagery, or other geospatial methods selected by the survey professional.

ENTRY 03 Compute & draft

Turn raw observations into coordinates, geometry, contours, and drawings.

Survey data is processed and checked before being converted into the plan, coordinate list, CAD drawing, surface model, technical description, or other deliverable required by the project.

ENTRY 04 Review & release

Deliver the right output for the client’s next decision.

Formal plans and submissions can require a registered and licensed Geodetic Engineer, appropriate reference systems, and agency-specific review. This concept avoids claiming client credentials until they are provided and verified.

PHILIPPINE PRACTICE NOTE

Surveying is professional work, not just a map graphic.

In the Philippines, geodetic engineering is a regulated profession. Formal survey work and plans may require the responsible registered and licensed Geodetic Engineer, appropriate survey references such as PRS92, and review by the relevant agency depending on the transaction. Final wording should be matched to the actual firm, licenses, services, and project jurisdiction before publication.
